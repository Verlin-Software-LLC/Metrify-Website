How it works

One application for the whole picture

Your diet, lifting routine, and body weight data finally live in the same place.

Published 31 July 2026

The fragmentation problem

Tracking your fitness usually involves a fragmented ecosystem of apps. You use one application for your food log, a second app to track your lifting sets, and a smartwatch to record your cardio and daily steps. You step on a smart scale that sends your weight to a health dashboard. None of these systems talk to each other in a meaningful way.

The cost of that fragmentation is not the extra icons on your home screen. It is that no single app can answer a real question. Your food app knows you ate 2,100 calories and has no idea you deadlifted. Your lifting app knows your working sets and nothing about whether you ate enough to recover. Your watch counted 11,000 steps and cannot tell you what that meant for your deficit.

Metrify puts these pieces together. It features chat-based logging for food, a dedicated Workout Lab for structured strength training with routines, sets, and reps, and integration with Apple Health and Google Health Connect. The app pulls in your weight, workouts, steps, and sleep, and you approve what gets logged.

Why the combination matters

The real benefit is that the assistant sees all of your data at once. An assistant answering questions needs your intake, your output, and your body-weight trend in the same place to say anything useful.

When your food and exercise are isolated, analyzing your progress requires manual reconciliation, which in practice means it does not happen. When they live in the same local database, you can see how your nutrition affects your lifting progression, or how your daily activity correlates with your weight trend. Ask why your weight stalled last week and the answer requires all three data sources at once.

Metrify also tracks micronutrients including fiber, sodium, and iron as part of this full picture, so the record covers more than the calorie total.

Weight trend and deceleration

Weight tracking requires careful mathematical handling. Day-to-day scale readings are mostly water, food volume, and timing, so a raw number is close to useless for judging progress. Metrify uses a 7-day smoothing average to filter out those fluctuations and reveal the actual trend underneath.

The weight projection is built on realistic metabolic behavior. As you lose mass, your maintenance calories fall, because a smaller body costs less energy to run. Metrify decays your maintenance requirement as your body mass changes, at roughly 12 calories per day for every kilogram lost.

This is why the projection curve flattens over time instead of continuing in a straight line. A projection that keeps predicting the same weekly loss forever is telling you something that cannot happen. Hold your intake constant while losing weight and your deficit shrinks on its own, until eventually it reaches zero and you plateau. Metrify shows you that curve rather than an optimistic straight line, so a slowdown three months out is something you planned for rather than something that blindsides you.

Honest limits

Metrify is designed for tracking and logging, not for comprehensive meal management. The application does not do meal planning, it does not support recipe import, and it does not generate grocery lists. It focuses on accurately recording what you did and analyzing the results, rather than planning what you will buy at the store.

If meal planning is the feature you most want, Metrify is not the right tool, and MyFitnessPal's Premium+ tier covers that ground properly. We would rather say so than have you find out after subscribing.

Summary

Metrify is a unified fitness tracker that combines conversational food logging, structured strength training routines, and health data synchronization in one application. It supports Apple Health and Google Health Connect for importing steps, workouts, sleep, and weight. The application uses 7-day smoothing for weight trends and reduces estimated maintenance calories as body mass changes to provide realistic weight projections.
